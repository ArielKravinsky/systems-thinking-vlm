In this study, two related but distinct objectives were evaluated: (a) constructing a scoring policy that rewards correct answers, and (b) assessing external validity against Questionnaire 1 at the participant level. The policy score was defined as a 50/50 composite of correctness and semantic similarity (SAC total vec-both). However, correlation analyses showed that these components were associated with Questionnaire 1 in different directions (correctness: negative trend; SAC similarity: positive trend), such that averaging them reduced the net association with the questionnaire. Accordingly, the 50/50 score should be interpreted as a design choice for balanced scoring rather than as the correlation-optimal predictor. To evaluate criterion alignment, we also conducted a weight sweep of composites X_w = w*Correct + (1-w)*SAC and estimated Pearson/Spearman associations with bootstrap confidence intervals. In the present sample, the strongest association was obtained at w = 0.00 (i.e., SAC-only), indicating that adding correctness improved policy interpretability but did not improve questionnaire alignment.

"We retained the 50/50 composite as the pre-defined policy score to reward correctness and semantic quality jointly, and additionally reported a criterion-optimized sensitivity analysis (weight sweep), which indicated maximal questionnaire alignment for SAC-only weighting in this dataset."
